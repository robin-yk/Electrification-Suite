Rate, transport, and velocity curvature, compared

Curvature determines whether pulsing provides a benefit

A two-state pulse spends fraction d at Tpeak and (1−d) at Tmin. Its time-average of any property f sits on the chord between the two endpoints; the continuous heater sits on the curve at Tavg. Whether the chord lies above or below the curve is determined entirely by the sign of f″.

The β slider functions as a sensitivity check. The underlying analysis proves h is concave for 0 < β < 1. Gas-phase diffusivity alone scales approximately as T1.75, so a transport coefficient dominated by diffusion may exceed β = 1, rendering h convex and offsetting part of the Λ gain. The validity of the Λ result therefore depends on the exponent applicable to a given reactor.

chord above

⟨k⟩ exceeds k(Tavg) whenever Ea > 2RT; the resulting gap represents the pulsing gain.

chord below

⟨h⟩ falls below h(Tavg) for β < 1, reducing the denominator of Λ.

⟨u⟩ equals u(Tavg) exactly; pulsing provides no benefit on the convective timescale.

Conditions under which pulsing provides a benefit

Both reported quantities are ratios of dimensionless groups, so their prefactors cancel: A drops out of Rexposure and RΛ, and h0/T0β drops out of RΛ. Only Ea, β, and the three temperatures survive, which is what makes the note's conclusion general rather than a property of one catalyst.

Duty-cycle dependence

At low duty cycle, most of the period is spent at Tmin, so Tavg decreases and the ratio against that reference increases. Absolute throughput declines concurrently, which the ratio does not capture.

The ratio alone does not determine practical benefit. The relative exposure ratio compares RPH against a CJH heater held at the RPH time-average temperature — that is, it demonstrates that pulsing outperforms a cooler continuous heater. It does not demonstrate that pulsing outperforms a continuous heater operated at Tpeak, which is the comparison relevant to an operator with sufficient power capacity. In that case, the argument for pulsing rests on selectivity and element lifetime, not on Λ or Da.

Equations, assumptions, and dimensional cancellation

All temperatures are converted to kelvin before entering any Arrhenius or power-law expression. The page reports ratios, so the pre-exponential A and the reference group h0/T0β cancel identically and are never needed as inputs.
Primary reference: Kwak, Y., Railkar, R., Zheng, W., & Vlachos, D. G. (2025). Tandem Nonoxidative Coupling of Methane and Carbon Dioxide Reduction via Pulsed Joule Thermochemistry. ACS Energy Letters, 10(12), 6188–6196. https://doi.org/10.1021/acsenergylett.5c02853. This page implements the RPH-vs-CJH dimensionless analysis developed there.

Constitutive forms

Rate constant
k(T) = A exp(−Ea / RT)
k″(T) = k(T) · Ea(Ea − 2RT) / (R2T4), so k is convex whenever Ea > 2RT. Default Ea = 273 kJ/mol, the lumped first-order ethane value.
Mass-transfer coefficient
h(T) = h0 (T / T0)β
h″(T) < 0 for 0 < β < 1. The slider spans past 1 so the concavity assumption can be stress-tested rather than assumed.
Velocity
u(T) = u0 (T / T0)
Ideal gas at fixed pressure and fixed molar flow. u″ = 0, so its time-average is exactly its average-temperature value.
Relative exposure
Rexposure = ⟨k(T)/u(T)⟩ / [k(Tavg)/u(Tavg)]
Residence time matters for conversion, but the page deliberately does not report absolute Da without a kinetic prefactor and a reactor model. Measured residence times remain in the experiment table.

Time-averaging

Continuous integration (animation tab)
⟨f⟩ = (1/t) ∫ f(T(t')) dt'
Accumulated numerically each animation frame over the real trapezoidal waveform, including the ramps. This is what the running-average charts plot.
Ideal two-state limit (ratios tab)
⟨f⟩ = d f(Tpeak) + (1 − d) f(Tmin)
Recovered when the ramp fraction goes to zero. This is the form the note proves against.
Reference temperature
Tavg = d Tpeak + (1 − d) Tmin
The CJH comparison is pinned here, so the two systems always share a time-average temperature.
The two ratios
RΛ = ⟨kL/h⟩ / [k(Tavg)L/h(Tavg)]
Rexposure = ⟨k/u⟩ / [k(Tavg)/u(Tavg)]
The ratios are dimensionless because the common characteristic length L cancels. The instantaneous k/u ratio is averaged directly; ⟨k/u⟩ cannot in general be replaced by ⟨k⟩/⟨u⟩.

Assumptions carried, and their cost

Single lumped rate
One Ea stands in for the whole radical network.
This is why the page never converts Da into a predicted conversion. Ethane cracking is chain-initiated with an induction period, and a lumped k significantly overpredicts conversion at millisecond contact times.
Gas follows the element instantly
Tgas = Telement with no lag.
A real pulse shorter than the gas thermal time constant is filtered, and the gas sees a smaller swing than the element. Short periods on the slider are therefore optimistic.
β treated as constant
One exponent across 400 to 1400 °C.
Flagged rather than defended. See the Λ caveat on the Jensen tab.
No coke, no element ageing
Properties are time-invariant.
Deposition and element degradation are the actual limits on a pulsed CFP heater and sit entirely outside this model.
