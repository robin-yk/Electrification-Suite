Methods named in the figures

Every numerical term that appears in a plate, stated without assuming finite-volume or Krylov background.

TermWhat it isWhere it appears
Finite volumeThe domain is cut into cells and an energy balance is written for each one, so whatever leaves a cell enters its neighbor exactly. There is one unknown temperature per cell.Fig. 3, Fig. 4
AxisymmetricSolved on an r–z half-plane and taken to be identical at every angle around the axis. Far cheaper than three dimensions, and unable to represent anything that varies with angle.Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3
Picard iterationThe material properties depend on temperature and the temperature depends on the properties. Guess a field, compute the coefficients from that guess, solve, then use the answer as the next guess. Repeat until the field stops moving. It replaces one nonlinear problem with a sequence of linear ones.Fig. 1
Under-relaxationMove only part of the way toward what the linear solve proposes, 0.62 of it here, so a strongly nonlinear case walks in rather than oscillating. The convergence test measures the full step, not the relaxed one.Fig. 1
PreconditioningRescale the system before solving it so the iterative solver converges in far fewer steps. Here each row is divided by its own diagonal entry, which is the Jacobi, or diagonal, preconditioner: the cheapest one there is.Fig. 1
PCGPreconditioned conjugate gradient. An iterative solver for a symmetric system that stores a handful of vectors and never forms the matrix. Run to a relative residual of 1×10−11.Fig. 1, Fig. 4
BiCGSTABBiconjugate gradient stabilized. Does the same job when the matrix is not symmetric, at more work per iteration. The code selects it only when one-sided entries are present and falls back to PCG when they are not, so the extra cost is paid only when it is needed.Fig. 1, Fig. 4
Symmetric operatorEntry (i,j) equals entry (j,i): if cell i acts on cell j, cell j acts back identically. Conduction and radiation are symmetric. A flow term that reads only the upstream cell is not, which is what forces the switch of solver.Fig. 4
Two-point fluxThe heat crossing a face is computed from the two cell-center temperatures alone, with the two half-cell resistances in series.Fig. 4
Backward EulerThe transient step writes the balance at the end of the step rather than the start. Stable at any step size and first-order accurate, so a step that is stable is not automatically a step that is accurate.Table S7
Unit-potential solveThe current-flow problem solved with one volt across the element. The distribution that comes out has the right shape and a meaningless magnitude, which is then fixed by scaling it to the known total power.Fig. 1, Fig. 5
Energy closureThe residual check on a converged solution: power in, minus power out, minus what went into storage, divided by the largest of the three. A correct solve returns a number near zero.Fig. 1
Gray-body pairRadiation between two surfaces whose emissivity does not vary with wavelength, with nothing absorbing in between. It is the simplest exchange model that conserves energy.Table S7
Plug flowThe gas moves at a single bulk velocity with no velocity profile across the gap. Only the enthalpy it carries is tracked; momentum is not solved.Fig. 4, Table S7
Far-field boundaryThe outer edge is held at a fixed condition and placed far enough out that moving it further does not change the answer.Fig. 3

Table S7. Questions the solver answers, and the boundary of each answer

Intended as Supporting Information Table S7. Every limitation below is a property of the model as implemented at solver commit {{COMMIT}}, not a statement about what a Joule-heated reactor does.

QuestionSupportedLimitation
What steady temperature does an element reach at a given current or power?YesGrid dependence must be checked. The 30 × 60 result differs from the finest-grid result by 0.60 K, equivalent to 0.07 % of the temperature rise.
How does temperature vary along the element and across its radius?YesAxisymmetric. Azimuthal variation, and any asymmetry in the enclosure or the flow, is outside the formulation.
Where in the element is the electrical power deposited?YesThe field solve returns a distribution only. Its integral is normalized to the scalar total, so a local hot spot redistributes power but cannot increase the power drawn.
How quickly does the element heat and cool under a pulsed drive?YesBackward Euler, first order in time and unconditionally stable. A step size that is stable is not automatically accurate, so the step must be refined and compared.
How is the heat lost, between radiation, convection and gas enthalpy?YesElement-to-wall exchange is a single gray-body pair. The gas is transparent; no participating-medium radiation is computed.
What does a rectangular element do?YesSolved through a cylinder that preserves length, radiating surface, mass and resistance. Volume is not preserved, and the radial geometry and conduction path differ from the strip, so the resulting field is an axisymmetric approximation to it.
How does the design scale to a multi-element reactor?In partOne element in one enclosure. Element-to-element radiation, manifold distribution and electrical balancing across a bank are outside the model.
What chemistry does the process gas undergo?NoThe gas carries sensible enthalpy only. No reaction, no species transport, no heat of reaction.
What is the flow field in the gas gap?NoA specified mass flow with plug-flow enthalpy transport. Momentum is not solved, and no velocity profile, pressure drop or recirculation is produced.
Will the element fail, oxidize or drift with age?NoCurrent density is reported against a stated material limit. There is no damage, oxidation or property-drift model.

Color and type conventions

One hue is assigned to one physical role and is used the same way in every plate. Each role carries three values derived from that hue: the hue itself for lines and data, a 14 % tint of it for fills, and a darkened shade for type, which amber and green need in order to hold contrast at 8 pt. Structure is not coloured: box outlines and connectors are a neutral grey, so a hue appears only where it names something. Where a panel shows two series of one role, as the two error norms and the two element temperatures in Fig. 3 do, they share the hue and are separated by marker. Figs. S7 and S8 carry no physical quantity, one being percentage deviations and the other a software structure, and so use the neutral and the ink alone. The set is colorblind-safe and stays legible in grayscale because the roles are also distinguished by position, by marker and by line style. Type is Arial throughout, no lighter than 8 pt at the printed size, on white with no shadow, gradient or transparency.
